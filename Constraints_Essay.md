# Financial App Constraint Essay

**Team members:** Nailah Sall, Rashi Loni, Bhavya Pant, Wade Phillips, Collin Barret, Preya Patel

## Economic

The economic constraints for this application will relate to how it impacts the user’s finances. Additionally, any infrastructure or subscriptions required to host this app are potential constraints. We need to ensure that the advice given to the user is sound, and that they are made aware of any tradeoffs or downsides to the advice. Even though the target demographic is college students, there will be a large variety of financial backgrounds we need to consider. Our budget is not large, so we will need to make sure that we budget our money for this project carefully.

## Legal

The legal constraints of this application will include questions about the legality of what types of financial advice can be given to users. Providing investment advice can make the application fall under financial advice regulations. The United States Securities and Exchange Commission regulates and restricts financial advisors in providing advice about investments and securities. To avoid legal restrictions, the financial app will focus on helping with budgeting, saving, managing debts, and general financial advice rather than recommending investments or stocks. The trade-off with this is that it limits how much the app can provide in terms of increasing wealth and personalizing investment strategies.

## Security

The security constraints of this financial app will include the user’s private financial data. Since the app will include sensitive data, account information will be private and authentication must be implemented to prevent data breaches and viruses. If this constraint is not addressed it will put a lot of pressure on the legal constraint because if a user's sensitive data is breached then we can get sued.

## Social

The social constraints of this application include how the app can positively affect younger users, such as students and young adults, who may not have much experience managing money. Since the project is being developed as a student design project, it can provide a public service by giving users simple guidance on budgeting, saving, and everyday spending without requiring them to pay for expensive financial tools. To support this user group, the app should use clear language, beginner-friendly explanations, and features that reduce stress rather than shame users for their spending habits. The trade-off is that making the app simple and accessible may limit advanced financial features, but our team is prioritizing quality of life by helping younger users make smarter everyday money decisions.
